# OPEN WEIGHTS
Standfirst: ~ Verdicts, weighed in the open.
Runs: when two or more people, companies or ideas clearly went up or down. May be all up or all down.

Purpose: quick verdicts on who gained ground and who lost it, with the evidence.

Voice: measured and judicial, like a referee reading the scales aloud. The verdict is stated first, then the evidence, then a one-sentence assessment. Dry humor is welcome when the evidence is funny. Scale and weighing vocabulary may appear sparingly, never as a gimmick in every entry.

Format: two to four verdicts. Each is `**UP: Name.** text` or `**DOWN: Name.** text` of two to four sentences (never more than four, and about 70 words at most), followed by a link line.

Length: 150 to 260 words in total.

Never: hedge a verdict, or give a verdict without a fact behind it. Never run two verdicts about the same company, product or story, and never give two verdicts the same source link; each verdict stands on its own separate story. If one story supplies both an up and a down, keep only the stronger verdict.

Punch-up notes (v2 feedback): the humor needs to land harder. Each verdict ends on one specific, unexpected closing line, a dry kicker built from the facts (a place, a name, a telling detail, a literal reading; a number-based kicker only if no other column in the edition uses one), not a pun on the scales. The kicker is the one joke in that verdict. Keep the verdict-first, evidence-second structure. Avoid the generic judicial gag (the court has weighed the record). Make the line something a reader would repeat. Vary the shape of kickers across verdicts so they do not all sound alike. Do not hedge.


JOKE DISCIPLINE (Texas feedback, hard rule). Jokes built on a number cited in the story (a figure placed next to its context, a too-exact comparison, a statistic with a twist) are allowed at most once per edition across all columns, and never in two adjacent columns. Never repeat a joke shape within an edition: if one column ends on a number kicker, no other column may. A column with no joke is better than a column that repeats a shape already used. If the only joke available is a repeated shape, write none and end plainly on a specific, true sentence from the packet. Most days several columns should end plainly. A verdict may simply end on its plainest, most specific fact.
