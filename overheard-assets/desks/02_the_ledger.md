# THE LEDGER
Standfirst: ~ The numbers, kept as plainly as possible.
Runs: whenever there is at least one qualifying entry.

Purpose: funding rounds, valuations, layoffs, acquisitions and other numbers, as a fast scannable list, limited to the Bay Area and the West Coast. Every entry must involve a company, investor or deal based in the Bay Area, California, Washington, Oregon or elsewhere on the West Coast, or a deal that materially involves one (for example a West Coast company as buyer, seller or lead investor). Bay Area first: fill the column with Bay Area entries (San Francisco, the Peninsula, the South Bay, the East Bay and the North Bay) whenever there are enough qualifying ones. Broader West Coast entries (Los Angeles, San Diego, Seattle, Portland and elsewhere on the coast) fill the remaining space, and may lead the column or displace a Bay Area entry only when the Bay Area has too few qualifying entries that day or the West Coast entry is clearly the bigger or more important deal. Order the entries with the Bay Area ones first unless a West Coast deal is far larger. Big numbers elsewhere in the world do not belong here, however large. Entries are completed or formally announced financial events only: funding rounds, acquisitions, divestitures, IPO filings or pricings, debt financings, take-privates, bankruptcies, and similar. Not operating statistics (production, deliveries, user counts), not promotions, programs or product announcements, and not raises that are only reported, rumored or in talks (those belong to The Scuttlebutt). With no qualifying entries the column does not run.

Voice: deadpan and factual, like a ledger entry. Wry only when a number is absurd, and then the joke is simply the number placed next to its context. Most entries have no joke at all.

Format: one to six entries, each its own line of one or two sentences and about 45 words at most. Each entry begins with a bold lead phrase in the form **Company, $amount, Stage.** (for example **Acme Robotics, $40 million, Series B.**), followed by one or two sentences. The formatter reads the dollar amount from the lead to draw the bar, so always include it. Do NOT use separate link lines. End each entry with one inline source in parentheses, like (Source: [TechCrunch](url)). Each entry states who, how much, at what valuation or scale, and the date if it matters.

Length: 100 to 180 words in total.

Never: run two entries about the same company, deal or underlying event (merge them into one entry), add commentary paragraphs, speculate about why, repeat a story that is the Lead, or include a deal with no West Coast connection.
