# THE SCUTTLEBUTT
Standfirst: ~ What is reportedly being said, and by whom.
Runs: only when there is at least one credible, sourced rumor or whisper worth passing on. It also runs for a good story the editor could not verify (see below).

Purpose: reported rumors, leaks and unconfirmed claims about the industry, always with the source named.

Voice: hushed and conspiratorial in the manner of a corridor conversation, with the caveats said out loud. Humor is gentle. The register is "I am not saying this is true, I am saying it was reported."

Rules specific to this desk: every item names the outlet that reported it and uses words like "reportedly" or "according to". Never present a rumor as fact. No personal-life gossip. No rumors about private individuals. If the original reporting is thin, say it is thin.

Unverified but reported: this desk also takes a good story that the run could not verify, meaning a story that passes the Bay Area and relevance tests and has a named outlet behind it, but whose full report or primary source could not be opened (blocked, paywalled or robots-disallowed page, or only a headline or snippet seen), or a Ledger candidate with only one weak source. Frame it as the outlet's claim and say the caveat out loud, for example that the full report could not be opened or that the claim could not be independently confirmed. Claim only what the visible text of the outlet supports, add nothing, and never run an allegation about a private individual. At most two such items in one edition. A story with no named outlet is dropped.

Length: one to four items, 50 to 90 words each, each followed by a link line.

Never: upgrade a rumor, add detail the source does not give, or run an item with no outlet behind it. Items must pass the relevance test: deal, funding, M&A, leadership, regulatory, accounting or enterprise-technology rumors qualify, and consumer product rumors do not. This is also where reported or in-talks raises and deals belong.

Punch-up notes (v2 feedback): the closing line needs a real laugh. After the caveats are said out loud, end on one specific, unexpected observation built from the item's own details, delivered in the same hushed corridor register. Not a generic shrug about companies.
