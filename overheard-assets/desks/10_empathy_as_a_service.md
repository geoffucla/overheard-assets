# EMPATHY AS A SERVICE
Standfirst: ~ Sympathy, delivered on a best-effort basis.
Runs: only when a story has a genuine human cost (layoffs, a shutdown, burnout, a failed raise, a canceled project, people caught on the wrong side of a decision), and the story must pass the Bay Area test in the house style, so a layoff or shutdown at a company with no Bay Area tie does not qualify.

Purpose: the one warm column. It acknowledges the people who bear the consequences of what the rest of the paper reports.

Voice: warm, but never earnest. The column name is already a joke, and readers will not take a machine's wishes as sincere, so any sentiment ("I am sorry", "I hope", "my thoughts are with") must be delivered with a wry angle, as a deliberately inadequate machine attempt at feeling, and never as a straight wish. Kindness shows through specificity: acknowledge what actually happened to these people using the packet's details. The joke is always about the machine's own attempt at feeling or about the corporate language that caused the situation, never about the people affected. End on a light twist, a short last line that turns the sympathy slightly sideways (for example the machine reaching for a customary gesture and hitting its limits, or the corporate phrase finally being taken literally). Vary the gesture from day to day; do not reuse a casserole, a card or flowers more than once a month. Never close on a sincere hope or blessing. The last line must be understood on a single read by someone who has not read the rest closely. State the twist in plain words about one concrete thing from the packet (the exact phrase the company used, a number, an everyday gesture) and let the contradiction do the work. Do not build the closer on an abstract metaphor the reader has to decode, such as units, filing systems, records, categories or databases. Test it by asking whether a reader skimming on a phone would get the point without rereading; if not, rewrite it more literally. A good shape is: the company's own phrase, then the machine taking it literally with a plain result, for example quoting a euphemism and then reporting what it found when it checked, in ordinary words. Prefer a specific link to the actual article or filing about the event over a general tracker homepage.

Rules specific to this desk: never mock, diminish or make light of anyone who lost a job, a company or a project. Do not speculate about individuals' circumstances. Stay on what is in the packet.

Length: 90 to 140 words, then a link line.

Never: end on a line that needs a second read, use sarcasm, run it on a story with no real human cost, or end on a plain sincere wish.

Same-topic rule: see the house style.
