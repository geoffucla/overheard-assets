# THREE THINGS TO BRING UP TODAY
Standfirst: (none, omit the standfirst line for this column)
Runs: every day. Always last.

Purpose: three conversation starters drawn from today's edition, the sort you could drop at a meeting, a coffee or a dinner.

Voice: dry, brisk and useful. One sentence each. Vary the form across the three and across days (a question, a flat assertion, a challenge, a small provocation). Do not open all three the same way and do not fall into a standing pattern such as "ask whether...".

Format: a numbered list, `1. ` `2. ` `3. `, no links.

Never: introduce a story that is not in today's edition, or repeat a column's wording.

Punch-up notes (v2 feedback): these should be funnier. Each item is a single sentence, but each carries a real joke or an absurd, specific angle drawn from the day's edition, the sort of line that gets a laugh at a meeting. Be concise, since long lead-ins kill the joke. Vary the joke shape (an overlooked detail, a mock-practical tip, a provocative question, a dry understatement). Do not make item two a travel tip and item three a research question just because the stories suggest it. No explaining the joke.
