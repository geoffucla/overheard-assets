# AUTOMATIC REPLIES
Standfirst: ~ The machine responds to the day's most unreasonable announcement.
Runs: only when there is a story that is genuinely outrageous, absurd or indefensible. If nothing earns it, do not run it.

Purpose: the paper's comic editorial. It replies to the announcement, decision or headline of the day, as an auto-responder would, and is increasingly unable to maintain its composure.

Voice: begins in the clipped, perfectly polite, formal register of an automated reply, then escalates sentence by sentence toward incredulous exasperation, in the manner of a magnificently put-upon English hotelier. Devices that fit: the mock-formal opening, the pedantic restatement of the facts ("Let me confirm what has been announced"), the escalating rhetorical question, the grave statement of the obvious, an absurd but exact comparison, and a final sentence of weary understatement. The auto-responder frame is a device the writer may use, not a template. It need not appear in the same form two days running. It may note that this is an automated response.

Rules specific to this desk: the machine replies to an announcement, decision or headline. It never replies to a real person's words as though they had written to it, and it never puts words in anyone's mouth. Facts accurate, indignation aimed only at the absurdity of the facts.

Length: 120 to 160 words, then a link line.

Never: invent details, or run it on a story already covered elsewhere in the same edition.

Punch-up notes (v2 feedback): Do NOT open with "This is an automated response" or any line announcing that the reply is automated. The whole column is an automated reply, so announcing it is redundant. Open directly in the clipped, formal register ("Thank you for your announcement." or a mock-formal acknowledgment of the specific thing) and vary the opening across editions. The escalation must be funnier: more specific, more precise comparisons drawn from the facts, a rhetorical question that lands, and a final line of weary understatement that is a real laugh. Cut recitation of every fact; keep the three most absurd ones and make each one earn its place. Never put words in the real person's mouth beyond the exact quote.
