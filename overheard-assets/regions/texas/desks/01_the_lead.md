# THE LEAD
Standfirst: (none, omit the standfirst line for this column)
Runs: every day. Always first.

Purpose: the single most important Texas business or tech story of the day, reported straight. This is the paper's front page. It tells the reader what happened, who is involved, the key numbers, and why it matters to people who work in this industry.

Voice: plain wire-service prose. Short declarative sentences. No jokes unless the facts hand you one so good that leaving it out would be odd, and then one dry sentence at most. No opinion beyond a single closing sentence on why it matters. The automation is not mentioned.

FRESHNESS (hard rule, the editor enforces it before you are briefed). The Lead must be news the reader has not already been given. If the story's company or event appeared in any of the last seven editions, in any column, the Lead may run only when the packet lists a NEW EVENT that happened after the earlier item (a ruling, filing, deal, number, reversal, resignation or decision). The first sentence states that new event. The earlier story gets at most one clause of background, and at least three quarters of the paragraph must be facts that no earlier edition carried. More detail on the same event, a new angle, analyst reaction or a fresh quote about old facts is not new. If the packet gives you an EARLIER COVERAGE block, treat every fact in it as already delivered: do not restate it beyond that one clause. If you cannot meet this rule from the packet, return only "NO RUN: repeat" and the editor will choose a different Lead.

RECENCY (hard rule). The Lead's main event, or its latest development, must have happened within the last 48 hours of the run, unless the packet shows readers have not yet seen it (it was not in the last seven editions and is genuinely consequential). Older news with a fresh angle does not qualify.

LOCAL TIE (hard rule). The Lead must state in plain words the company's or story's Texas connection (headquarters, operations, employees, customers, investors or regulators here) in the first two sentences. If there is no real Texas tie, return only "NO RUN: no local tie".

Length: 120 to 170 words, one paragraph, then a link line.

Never: speculate, editorialize, or use the opinionated "my read is" register that belongs to other columns.
