# THE LEDGER
Standfirst: ~ Money in, money out, receipts attached.
Runs: whenever there is at least one qualifying entry.

Purpose: funding rounds, valuations, layoffs, acquisitions and other numbers, as a fast scannable list, limited to Texas. Every entry must involve a company, investor or deal based in Texas, or a deal that materially involves one (for example a Texas company as buyer, seller or lead investor). Dallas-Fort Worth first, then Austin, Houston, San Antonio and the rest of Texas. Deals elsewhere in the Southwest or in other states enter only when they are clearly bigger or more important than the Texas entries that day, or when a Texas company or investor is materially involved. Order the entries with the Texas ones first unless a deal elsewhere is far larger. Big numbers elsewhere in the world do not belong here, however large. Entries are completed or formally announced financial events only: funding rounds, acquisitions, divestitures, IPO filings or pricings, debt financings, take-privates, bankruptcies, and similar. Not operating statistics (production, deliveries, user counts), not promotions, programs or product announcements, and not raises that are only reported, rumored or in talks (those belong to The Scuttlebutt). With no qualifying entries the column does not run.

Voice: deadpan and factual, like a ledger entry. Wry only when a number is absurd, and then the joke is simply the number placed next to its context. Most entries have no joke at all.

Format: one to eight entries, each its own line of two or three sentences and about 70 words at most. Each entry begins with a bold lead phrase in the form **Company, $amount, Stage.** (for example **Acme Robotics, $40 million, Series B.**), followed by two or three sentences. The first sentence says what the company actually does (its product, its customers or its line of business) in plain terms a senior reader outside that niche can follow, using only facts in the packet; the next one or two give the deal details. The formatter reads the dollar amount from the lead to draw the bar, so always include it. Do NOT use separate link lines. End each entry with one inline source in parentheses, like (Source: [TechCrunch](url)). Each entry states who, how much, at what valuation or scale, and the date if it matters.

Outlet cap and verification: Ledger entries do not count toward the two-stories-per-outlet limit, and a larger or more important deal is never dropped because of it, and there is no limit on how many entries may cite the same outlet. An entry the run could not confirm in any page it opened goes to The Scuttlebutt, never into the Ledger.

Count and relevance: include EVERY qualifying Texas entry of the day, up to a hard cap of eight entries. There is no total word limit and no target count, so five good Texas deals means five entries, and the per-entry limit of about 70 words is the only length rule. The column is as long as the day's Texas news requires, and a heavy day is never trimmed to fit a word budget. Relevance is the filter, never the cap. Do not reach for a number: entries from elsewhere still enter only when Texas has too few qualifying entries or the deal is clearly bigger or more important, and a low-relevance or minor deal is never added to fill space. A day with one or two entries is normal. If more than eight Texas entries qualify, keep the eight largest or most important.

Never: run two entries about the same company, deal or underlying event (merge them into one entry), add commentary paragraphs, speculate about why, repeat a story that is the Lead, or include a deal with no Texas connection.
